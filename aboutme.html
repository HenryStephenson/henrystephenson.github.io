University

I started at McGill back in 2021 at the tail end of the COVID-19 pandemic. Entering in Bioengineering, I had no clue what I was doing. I was lucky enough to receive a Prestige Scholarship (the JW McConnell Award), making the finances of university less strenuous than for others, though for the first time I was no longer a big fish in a small pond. Suddenly, direction was something I seemed to lack compared to my many private school colleauges with substantial preparedness and direction from parents, school, and their community. Growing up in a small town has some benefits, but an inherent "push" from your community for greatness is generally not one: it has to be derived from within. After one egregious calculus midterm grade, I was set in motion.

After turning around my calculus performance and ending my first year with a perfect 4.0 GPA, I found passion for disease research at Ostrowski Lab at U of T. At the time, I was especially interested in viruses and thought I'd pursue an HIV project with Ostrowski Lab. Getting my first laboratory training and contributing significantly to a project that would be eventually published in Cell gave me a taste for something over which I came to obsess.
